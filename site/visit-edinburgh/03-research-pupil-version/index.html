    Lesson Three - Research

    Today You Will

    By the end of this lesson, you will have research notes ready for your Visit Edinburgh website.

    In this lesson, research means looking for useful information that could help someone visiting Edinburgh.

    You are not trying to find everything. You are trying to find information that would help a visitor.

    You will:

    • Choose one Edinburgh place or topic.
    • Find one useful fact.
    • Check where the fact came from.
    • Rewrite the fact in your own words.
    • Save the source URL so you can use it later.

    What Good Research Looks Like

    Research is the work you do before you write your website.

    It means:

    1. Find information.
    2. Choose the useful parts.
    3. Check where the information came from.
    4. Rewrite it so it fits your website.

    Here is one finished example.

    What I Need My Research Note
    Place or topic Edinburgh Castle
    Useful fact It has exhibitions, city views, and important Scottish history.
    Source website VisitScotland
    Source URL https://www.visitscotland.com/
    My website sentence Edinburgh Castle is a historic place to visit, with exhibitions, city views, and important Scottish history.

    This is useful because a visitor can quickly understand why they might want to go there.

    Keywords For Today

    Keyword Simple Meaning
    Research Looking for useful information before you make something.
    Source The place where you found the information or image.
    Reliable Something you can trust because it is likely to be accurate.
    Source URL The web address of the page you used.
    Rewrite Put the information into your own words.

    You do not need to memorise these before you start. Use them as you work.

    What Is A Source?

    A source is where your information came from.

    It could be:

    • A website
    • A map
    • A museum page
    • A transport page
    • An official tourism page
    • A photo website

    If you found a fact on the VisitScotland website, then VisitScotland is your source.

    If you found an image on a museum website, then the museum website is your image source.

    Why Does The Source Matter?

    The source matters because visitors need information they can trust.

    For example:

    Information Source Would I Trust It?
    Edinburgh Castle is a popular historic attraction. VisitScotland Yes, because it is an official tourism website.
    Edinburgh Castle is boring and nobody visits it. A random comment online No, because it is only one person's opinion.

    Good sources help your website feel more accurate and professional.

    Why Do I Need The Source Address?

    The source address means the web address, also called the source URL.

    It usually starts with https://.

    For example:

    https://www.visitscotland.com/

    You need the source address because it lets you:

    • Find the same information again later.
    • Prove where your fact came from.
    • Check that the fact has not been guessed.
    • Give credit for information or images.
    • Fix mistakes if the information changes.

    Think of the source address like writing down where you found something. If you do not save it, you may not be able to find it again.

    Step 1 - Choose Something To Research

    Choose one place or topic that could appear on your website.

    Examples:

    • Edinburgh Castle
    • National Museum of Scotland
    • Princes Street Gardens
    • Getting around Edinburgh
    • Food near the city centre
    • Things for families to do

    Write your choice:

    Question My Answer
    What am I researching?

    Step 2 - Find One Useful Fact

    A useful fact helps the visitor make a decision.

    Weak Fact Better Fact
    Edinburgh Castle is good. Edinburgh Castle has exhibitions, city views, and important Scottish history.
    The museum is big. The National Museum of Scotland has galleries about nature, science, history, and world cultures.
    There are buses. Edinburgh has buses and trams that help visitors travel around the city.

    Now find one useful fact for your own website.

    Question My Answer
    What useful fact did I find?

    Step 3 - Check The Source

    Ask yourself: would I trust this website for visitor information?

    Remember: the source is where the information came from.

    Good sources are often:

    • Official tourism websites
    • Museum or attraction websites
    • Council or transport websites
    • Websites with clear information and contact details

    Be careful with websites that:

    • Have lots of pop-ups or adverts
    • Do not say who wrote the information
    • Look like they are only trying to sell something
    • Have strange or unclear web addresses
    Source Would I Trust It? Why?
    www.visitscotland.com Yes It is an official tourism website.
    www.best-holidays-and-discounts123.biz No The address looks unclear and less professional.

    Now check your source.

    Question My Answer
    What website did I use?
    What is the source URL? Copy the web address from the address bar.
    Why do I trust or not trust it?

    Step 4 - Rewrite The Fact

    Do not copy a whole sentence straight from the website.

    Read the fact, think about what it means, then write it in your own words.

    Source Fact Rewritten For A Website
    Edinburgh Castle is located on Castle Rock and is one of Scotland's most famous historic sites. Edinburgh Castle sits high on Castle Rock and is one of Scotland's most famous historic places to visit.

    Now rewrite your fact.

    Question My Answer
    My rewritten sentence

    Put It All Together

    Complete one row first. Then add more rows if you have time.

    The source website tells you who or what gave you the information.

    The source URL tells you exactly where to find it again.

    Place or Topic Useful Fact Source Website Source URL My Website Sentence
    Edinburgh Castle It has exhibitions, city views, and important Scottish history. VisitScotland https://www.visitscotland.com/ Edinburgh Castle is a historic place to visit, with exhibitions, city views, and important Scottish history.

    Your Task

    Start with Level One. Move up when you are ready.

    Complete one box in the research table.

    You could start with the place or topic.

    Complete one full row in the research table.

    Make sure you include the source website and source URL.

    Complete two full rows in the research table.

    Each row should include a rewritten sentence for your website.

    Complete three or more full rows in the research table.

    Check that each fact is useful for your chosen audience.

    Choose your best row and explain why that information would help a visitor.

    Quick Check

    Before you finish, check:

    • Did I choose information that helps a visitor?
    • Did I write down the website name?
    • Did I copy the source URL?
    • Did I rewrite the fact in my own words?
    • Would I trust the source?
