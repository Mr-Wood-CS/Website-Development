Planning a One Page Website

Today

Plan a one-page Visit Edinburgh website.

We will explore how to organise a website so that visitors find the information they need quickly and easily.

  • Identify the target audience and the website's main purpose.
  • Choose the best sections for a one-page design.
  • Create a clear page plan with headings and content order.

Key Words

Audience
Who the website is designed for, such as tourists or families.
Purpose
Why the site exists: to inform, persuade, help visitors, or encourage booking.
Section
A block of related content, for example attractions, transport or tips.
Layout
How the page is arranged so it is easy to read and use.
Information
The facts, details and messages the visitor needs.

Website Questions

  • Who is it for? - Choose a specific audience so your content and images are relevant.
  • What does the user need? - Think about the visitor’s main goal, such as planning a trip or finding attractions.
  • What information comes first? - Put the most important details at the top of the page.
  • What action should the user take? - Decide whether the site guides the visitor to explore, book, or contact someone.

Model Structure

This wireframe shows a simple one-page layout with sections arranged in a logical order.

  • Title: A strong heading and image to welcome visitors.
  • Introduction: A short overview of what the page is about.
  • Attractions: Key places to visit in Edinburgh.
  • Transport: How to get to and around the city.
  • Visitor tips: Practical advice for a better trip.
  • Credits: Sources, image credits, and any extra information.

Your Task

Complete the Website Brief and Page Plan Storyboard.

  • Website Brief: Write who the site is for, what it should do, and what content it needs.
  • Page Plan Storyboard: Arrange the sections, headings and images in the order visitors will read them.
  • Use labels and notes to explain why each section is included and how it helps the user.

Exit

Audience is who the website is for. Purpose is what the website is trying to do.

Check your plan against these points:

  • Is the audience clearly defined?
  • Does the page meet the visitor’s main needs?
  • Are sections ordered logically?
  • Does it show the action the visitor should take?
