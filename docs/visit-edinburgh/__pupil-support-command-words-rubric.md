# :material-stairs: Describe And Explain Answer Levels

This page helps you see how an answer can move from Level One to Level Four.

The question stays the same.

The answer gets better because it adds:

- Clearer detail
- Better vocabulary
- Examples
- Reasons
- Impact on the website or visitor

---

## :material-format-list-bulleted-type: Describe Questions

A **describe** question asks you to say what something is like.

Try to include:

- What it is
- Useful detail
- An example if it helps

### Focus Question

**Describe what a source is.**

=== "Level One"

    A source is a website.

    This is a start, but it is very short.

=== "Level Two"

    A source is where information comes from.

    This says what a source is, but it could still give more detail.

=== "Level Three"

    A source is where information or an image came from, such as a website, map, book, or official page.

    This is clearer because it gives examples.

=== "Level Four"

    A source is where information or an image originally came from, such as a website, map, book, or official page. When making a visitor website, sources are used to collect facts and images that help the audience.

    This is stronger because it describes the term and links it to the website task.

## :material-lightbulb-outline: Explain Questions

An **explain** question asks you to give a reason why something matters.

Try to include:

- A clear point
- The word **because**
- What happens as a result
- Why it matters for the website or visitor

### Focus Question

**Explain why saving the source URL is important.**

=== "Level One"

    So you know where it came from.

    This gives a basic idea, but it does not explain enough.

=== "Level Two"

    Saving the source URL is important because it shows where the information came from.

    This gives a reason using **because**.

=== "Level Three"

    Saving the source URL is important because it shows exactly where the information came from. This means you can find the same information again later.

    This is clearer because it gives a reason and a result.

=== "Level Four"

    Saving the source URL is important because it shows exactly where the information came from. This means you can check the information again later, prove that it was not guessed, and give credit for facts or images used on your website.

    This is stronger because it explains several reasons and links them to careful website research.

---

## :material-compare: Describe Or Explain?

Look at the command word first.

| Command Word | Your Job | Helpful Starter |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Describe | Say what it is like | `A source is...` |
| Explain | Give a reason why it matters | `This is important because...` |

## :memo: Your Turn

Choose one question.

The question stays the same. Your answer shows the level you are working at.

=== "Question 1"

    **Describe what research means.**

    Level One: Research means finding information.

    Level Two: Research means finding useful information before making something.

    Level Three: Research means finding useful information before making something, such as facts for a website.

    Level Four: Research means finding useful information before making something. For the `Visit Edinburgh` website, research helps you choose facts that would help visitors plan their trip.

=== "Question 2"

    **Explain why facts should be rewritten in your own words.**

    Level One: So you do not copy.

    Level Two: Facts should be rewritten in your own words because copying is not original.

    Level Three: Facts should be rewritten in your own words because copying is not original. This also helps you show that you understand the information.

    Level Four: Facts should be rewritten in your own words because copying is not original. This also helps you show that you understand the information and lets you make the sentence clearer for your website audience.

=== "Question 3"

    **Explain why reliable sources help your website.**

    Level One: They make it better.

    Level Two: Reliable sources help your website because they are more likely to be accurate.

    Level Three: Reliable sources help your website because they are more likely to be accurate. Poor sources might include mistakes or outdated information.

    Level Four: Reliable sources help your website because they are more likely to be accurate. Poor sources might include mistakes or outdated information, which could make visitors trust the website less.

## :material-check-circle-outline: Quick Check

Before you finish, check:

- Did I answer the command word?
- Did I add enough detail for my chosen level?
- For describe, did I say what it is like?
- For explain, did I give a reason using **because**?
- Did I link my answer to the website or visitor if I am aiming for Level Four?
