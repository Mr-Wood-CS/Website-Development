# :material-help-circle-outline: Answering Explain Questions

This page is here to help you answer **explain** questions in Computing Science.

An **explain** question asks you to give a reason why something matters, happens, or is useful.

---

## :material-lightbulb-outline: What Does Explain Mean?

An **explain** question asks you to give a reason.

When you explain, you should:

- Make a clear point
- Give a reason why
- Add detail to show understanding
- Use words such as **because**, **so**, **therefore**, or **this means that**

An explain answer should not just say what something is. It should help the reader understand **why** it matters.

**Possible Explain Sentence Starters**

You could start an explain answer with:

- `This is important because...`
- `This helps because...`
- `This means that...`
- `As a result...`
- `The reason for this is...`
- `This would improve the website because...`

---

=== "Example 1"

    | Part | Example |
    |---|---|
    | Question | **Explain why facts should be rewritten in your own words.** |
    | Weak Answer | So you do not copy. |
    | Why This Is Weak | This answer gives a small reason, but it does not explain the reason clearly enough. |
    | Better Answer | Facts should be rewritten in your own words because copying directly from a source can make the website less original and harder to understand. Rewriting facts helps you show that you understand the information. It also lets you make the text clearer and more suitable for your own website audience. |
    | Why This Is Better | Uses the word because, gives more than one reason, links the answer to the final website, and explains how rewriting helps the audience. |

=== "Example 2"

    | Part | Example |
    |---|---|
    | Question | **Explain why recording the source URL is important.** |
    | Weak Answer | So you know where it came from. |
    | Why This Is Weak | This answer is on the right track, but it needs more detail. |
    | Better Answer | Recording the source URL is important because it shows exactly where the information came from. This means the information can be checked again later to make sure it is accurate. It also helps prove that the research came from a suitable source rather than being guessed or copied without care. |
    | Why This Is Better | Gives a clear reason, explains why checking later is useful, links source recording to accuracy, and shows why careful research matters. |

=== "Example 3"

    | Part | Example |
    |---|---|
    | Question | **Explain why the quality of your sources affects the final website.** |
    | Weak Answer | Because bad sources are bad. |
    | Why This Is Weak | This answer repeats the question and does not give a proper reason. |
    | Better Answer | The quality of sources affects the final website because reliable sources help make the information accurate and trustworthy. If poor sources are used, the website may include mistakes, outdated facts, or misleading information. This could make visitors trust the website less and make the website less useful. |
    | Why This Is Better | Explains the impact of reliable sources, explains the problem with poor sources, links source quality to visitor trust, and shows how research affects the final product. |

## :material-table-check: Describe or Explain?

| Command Word | What It Means | What Your Answer Should Do |
|---|---|---|
| Describe | Say what something is like | Give details and examples |
| Explain | Give reasons why something matters or happens | Use because, so, therefore, or this means that |

---

## :material-comment-check-outline: Reflection

Before moving on, make sure you can:

- Explain what an explain question is asking you to do
- Use words such as **because** and **this means that**
- Give a clear reason in your answer
- Add enough detail to show understanding
- Explain why something matters, is useful, or happens
