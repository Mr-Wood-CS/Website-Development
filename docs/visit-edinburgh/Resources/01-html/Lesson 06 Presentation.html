Lesson 06

Final Assessment and Evaluation

Submit the website and explain how well it meets the brief.

Today's Lesson

Submit and evaluate

Learning Intention

We will submit a final one-page website and evaluate it using evidence from planning, building, testing, and refinement.

Success Criteria

  • Complete final checks before submission.
  • Identify evidence that shows the website meets the brief.
  • Evaluate strengths and areas for improvement.
  • Link design decisions to audience, purpose, and user experience.

A strong evaluation uses evidence, not just opinion.

Key Words

Words we will use today

Evaluation

Judging the quality of the final website using evidence.

Evidence

Proof from planning, research, testing, feedback, or the final product.

Success criteria

The points used to judge whether the work has met the task.

Refinement

A change made to improve the website after testing.

Next step

Something that could be improved in a future version.

Use these words when explaining the quality of your final website.

Evidence

What proves the website meets the brief?

Context

You are preparing to submit your Visit Edinburgh website. You need evidence from across the project.

  • Planning Website brief, audience, purpose, storyboard, and section order.
  • Research Research log, source notes, image credits, and information model.
  • Website The final one-page RocketCake website.
  • Testing Test plan, feedback, issues found, fixes made, and retesting.

Evidence helps you prove that the final website did not happen by accident.

Rubric

What will quality look like?

AreaWhat to look for
DesignLayout, readability, section order, and visual consistency.
ContentUseful information for the audience and clear source awareness.
Technical useEffective use of RocketCake tools.
TestingEvidence of checks, feedback, fixes, and retesting.
EvaluationClear judgement linked to evidence.
Computing understandingAudience, purpose, structured information, and user experience.

The rubric shows what to evidence, not just what to finish.

Evaluation

Explain decisions using evidence

Context

Your evaluation should show what worked, what changed, and what you would improve next.

  • Strength What works well? What evidence proves this?
  • Decision Which design choice helped the audience or purpose?
  • Refinement What did you change after testing or feedback?
  • Next step What would you improve if you had more time?

Avoid "I like it" on its own. Explain what works and why.

Final Task

Submit and evaluate

  1. Complete final checks on the website.
  2. Make sure sources and image credits are included.
  3. Save/export/submit the final website as instructed.
  4. Gather evidence from planning, research, building, and testing.
  5. Write an evaluation using the success criteria.
  6. Identify one realistic next step.
Support

One strength is ___ because the evidence shows ___.

Challenge

Evaluate whether your evidence is strong enough and identify gaps.

Check for Understanding

Scenario

You are evaluating your final Visit Edinburgh website and deciding what evidence proves its quality.

Choose the level that stretches your thinking

1Beginning to learn
  1. Name one strength.
  2. Name one target.
2Making progress
  1. Match one evidence item to one success criterion.
  2. Choose one computing word that explains your work.
3Making good progress
  1. Explain how one piece of evidence demonstrates quality.
  2. Explain how testing improved the final version.
4Meeting target
  1. Evaluate whether your evidence is sufficient.
  2. Justify one future improvement target.

Start where you feel confident, then try the next level.

Exit Ticket

What would you improve next time?

1Beginning to learn
  1. Next time I would improve ___.
2Making progress
  1. Next time I would improve ___ because ___.
3Making good progress
  1. Next time I would improve ___ because ___. I would do this by ___.
4Meeting target
  1. This would improve user experience by ___.

Evaluation helps you explain quality and improve future projects.
