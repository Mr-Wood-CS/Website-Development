# Lesson 06 Presentation: Final Assessment and Evaluation

## Slide 1: Title

**Kicker:** Lesson 06  
**Title:** Final Assessment and Evaluation  
**Subtitle:** Submit the website and explain how well it meets the brief.

**Purpose:** Guide pupils through final checks, submission evidence, rubric thinking, and reflective evaluation.

## Slide 2: Learning Intention and Success Criteria

**Learning intention:**  
We will submit a final one-page website and evaluate it using evidence from planning, building, testing, and refinement.

**Success criteria:**
- I can complete final checks before submission.
- I can identify evidence that shows my website meets the brief.
- I can evaluate strengths and areas for improvement.
- I can link design decisions to audience, purpose, and user experience.

**Footer prompt:**  
A strong evaluation uses evidence, not just opinion.

## Slide 3: Key Words

**Kicker:** Key Words  
**Title:** Words we will use today

**Keyword cards:**

**Evaluation**  
Judging the quality of the final website using evidence.

**Evidence**  
Proof from planning, research, testing, screenshots, feedback, or the final product.

**Success criteria**  
The points used to judge whether the work has met the task.

**Refinement**  
A change made to improve the website after testing or feedback.

**Next step**  
Something that could be improved in a future version.

**Footer prompt:**  
Use these words when explaining the quality of your final website.

## Slide 4: Evidence

**Kicker:** Evidence  
**Title:** What proves the website meets the brief?

**Context:**  
You are preparing to submit your Visit Edinburgh website. You need evidence from across the project, not just the final page.

**Evidence cards:**

**Planning**  
Website brief, audience, purpose, storyboard, and section order.

**Research**  
Research log, source notes, image credits, and information model.

**Website**  
The final one-page RocketCake website with clear content and layout.

**Testing**  
Test plan, peer feedback, issues found, fixes made, and retest evidence.

**Evaluation**  
A written judgement explaining strengths, improvements, and next steps.

**Side note:**  
Evidence helps you prove that the final website did not happen by accident.

## Slide 5: Rubric

**Kicker:** Rubric  
**Title:** What will quality look like?

**Assessment areas:**

| Area | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Design | Layout, readability, section order, and visual consistency |
| Content | Useful information for the audience and clear source awareness |
| Technical use | Effective use of RocketCake tools |
| Testing | Evidence of checks, feedback, fixes, and retesting |
| Evaluation | Clear judgement linked to evidence |
| Computing understanding | Audience, purpose, structured information, and user experience |

**Footer prompt:**  
The rubric shows what to evidence, not just what to finish.

## Slide 6: Strong Evaluation

**Kicker:** Evaluation  
**Title:** Explain decisions using evidence

**Context:**  
Your evaluation should show what worked, what changed, and what you would improve next.

**Evaluation prompts:**

**Strength**  
What works well in the final website? What evidence proves this?

**Decision**  
Which design choice helped the audience or purpose?

**Refinement**  
What did you change after testing or feedback?

**Next step**  
What would you improve if you had more time?

**Side note:**  
Avoid "I like it" on its own. Explain what works and why.

## Slide 7: Your Task

**Kicker:** Final Task  
**Title:** Submit and evaluate

**Task steps:**
1. Complete final checks on the website.
2. Make sure sources and image credits are included.
3. Save/export/submit the final website as instructed.
4. Gather evidence from planning, research, building, and testing.
5. Write an evaluation using the success criteria.
6. Identify one realistic next step.

**Support:**  
Use: "One strength is ___ because the evidence shows ___."

**Challenge:**  
Evaluate whether your evidence is strong enough and identify any gaps.

## Slide 8: Check for Understanding

**Kicker:** Check for Understanding  
**Title:** Choose the level that stretches your thinking

**Scenario:**  
You are evaluating your final Visit Edinburgh website and deciding what evidence proves its quality.

**1 - Beginning to learn**
- Name one strength of your website.
- Name one target for improvement.

**2 - Making progress**
- Match one piece of evidence to one success criterion.
- Choose one computing word that explains your design work.

**3 - Making good progress**
- Explain how one piece of evidence demonstrates quality.
- Explain how your testing improved the final version.

**4 - Meeting target**
- Evaluate whether your evidence is sufficient and identify any gaps.
- Justify one improvement target for a future project.

**Footer prompt:**  
Start where you feel confident, then try the next level.

## Slide 9: Exit Ticket

**Kicker:** Exit Ticket  
**Title:** What would you improve next time?

**1 - Beginning to learn**  
Complete: Next time I would improve ___.

**2 - Making progress**  
Complete: Next time I would improve ___ because ___.

**3 - Making good progress**  
Complete: Next time I would improve ___ because ___. I would do this by ___.

**4 - Meeting target**  
Complete: Next time I would improve ___ because ___. I would do this by ___. This would improve user experience by ___.

**Final takeaway:**  
Evaluation helps you explain the quality of your work and how you would improve future projects.
