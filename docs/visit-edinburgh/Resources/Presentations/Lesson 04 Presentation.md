# Lesson 04 Presentation: Layout, Navigation and Devices

## Slide 1: Title

**Kicker:** Lesson 04  
**Title:** Layout, Navigation and Devices  
**Subtitle:** Improve structure, readability, and how the page works for different users.

**Purpose:** Help pupils refine the draft website so information is easier to understand and navigate.

## Slide 2: Learning Intention and Success Criteria

**Learning intention:**  
We will improve the structure and readability of a one-page website and check how it appears on different devices.

**Success criteria:**
- I can improve headings, spacing, and section order.
- I can explain how layout affects user understanding.
- I can preview the page on different device sizes.
- I can make navigation choices that support the audience.

**Footer prompt:**  
Good layout helps visitors understand the page quickly.

## Slide 3: Key Words

**Kicker:** Key Words  
**Title:** Words we will use today

**Keyword cards:**

**Heading**  
A clear label that tells users what a section is about.

**Whitespace**  
Empty space around content that helps the page feel readable and organised.

**Navigation**  
How users move through the website or find the section they need.

**Device**  
The screen used to view the page, such as a phone, tablet, laptop, or desktop monitor.

**Responsive layout**  
A layout that changes or adapts so the same information can be used on different screens.

**Footer prompt:**  
Use these words when explaining how your layout helps users.

## Slide 4: Structured Information

**Kicker:** Structured Information  
**Title:** Headings and sections help users scan the page

**Context:**  
Your one-page website has several sections. Visitors should be able to understand what each section is for without reading everything first.

**Question cards:**

**Are headings clear?**  
Each heading should tell the user what information comes next.

**Are sections grouped well?**  
Related text, images, and facts should sit together.

**Is spacing consistent?**  
Similar sections should have similar spacing so the page feels organised.

**Can users scan quickly?**  
Important information should be easy to find without searching through long paragraphs.

**Side note:**  
Structure makes information easier to understand.

## Slide 5: Devices

**Kicker:** Device Preview  
**Title:** The same webpage can look different on different screens

**Context/scenario:**  
Your website might be viewed on a phone, tablet, laptop, or desktop monitor. The content is the same, but the layout may change.

**Comparison points:**

**Phone**  
Content may stack vertically and images may take up more screen height.

**Tablet**  
Some sections may fit side by side, but spacing still needs checking.

**Desktop**  
There is more horizontal space, but wide lines of text can become harder to read.

**Footer prompt:**  
Previewing helps you spot layout problems before users do.

## Slide 6: Navigation

**Kicker:** Navigation  
**Title:** One-page websites still need a clear route

**Navigation checks:**
- Does the first section introduce the page clearly?
- Does the section order match the visitor's needs?
- Would anchor links or a section menu help?
- Are credits and sources easy to find?
- Does the page ending feel complete?

**Side note:**  
Navigation is not only menus. It is also the order and clarity of the page.

## Slide 7: Your Task

**Kicker:** Main Task  
**Title:** Improve the layout and preview the page

**Task steps:**
1. Check that every section has a clear heading.
2. Improve spacing between sections.
3. Make images and text easier to read.
4. Preview the page on at least two device sizes.
5. Improve one part of the navigation or section order.
6. Record what changed and why it helped the user.

**Support:**  
Choose one section and improve only its heading, spacing, and image placement first.

**Challenge:**  
Compare the page on two devices and justify one layout improvement for your audience.

## Slide 8: Check for Understanding

**Kicker:** Check for Understanding  
**Title:** Choose the level that stretches your thinking

**Scenario:**  
You are reviewing your Visit Edinburgh page on different devices and deciding which layout changes will help visitors most.

**1 - Beginning to learn**
- What does a browser do?
- Name one part of a web address.

**2 - Making progress**
- Give two ways the same information could be represented on a webpage.
- Define sender and receiver.

**3 - Making good progress**
- Explain sender, receiver, address, and packets in simple terms.
- Explain how these ideas relate to loading your webpage.

**4 - Meeting target**
- Explain how structured information and network processes both affect user experience.
- Evaluate one navigation choice in your page and justify whether it should stay or change.

**Footer prompt:**  
Start where you feel confident, then try the next level.

## Slide 9: Exit Ticket

**Kicker:** Exit Ticket  
**Title:** What changed and why did it help?

**1 - Beginning to learn**  
Describe one layout change you made.

**2 - Making progress**  
Describe one layout change and why you made it.

**3 - Making good progress**  
Describe one layout change and explain how it helped readability.

**4 - Meeting target**  
Describe one layout change and evaluate its impact on users on different devices.

**Final takeaway:**  
The same information can be represented differently depending on layout, device, and user need.
