# Lesson 05 Presentation: Testing and Refinement

## Slide 1: Title

**Kicker:** Lesson 05  
**Title:** Testing and Refinement  
**Subtitle:** Use evidence and feedback to improve the final website.

**Purpose:** Help pupils test their website, collect useful feedback, and make justified improvements.

## Slide 2: Learning Intention and Success Criteria

**Learning intention:**  
We will test a one-page website, use feedback, and refine it to improve usability and accessibility.

**Success criteria:**
- I can test my website against requirements.
- I can identify readability, link, image, and accessibility issues.
- I can use peer feedback to choose improvements.
- I can explain how a change improved the user experience.

**Footer prompt:**  
Testing is useful when it leads to a specific improvement.

## Slide 3: Key Words

**Kicker:** Key Words  
**Title:** Words we will use today

**Keyword cards:**

**Testing**  
Checking whether the website works, meets the brief, and is suitable for users.

**Accessibility**  
Designing so more people can read, understand, and use the website.

**Feedback**  
Information from another person that helps the designer improve the work.

**Refinement**  
A change made to improve the quality of the website.

**Evidence**  
Proof that a test was carried out or an improvement was made.

**Footer prompt:**  
Use these words when explaining your improvements.

## Slide 4: Types of Test

**Kicker:** Testing Questions  
**Title:** Choose tests that reveal useful problems

**Context:**  
You are testing the first draft of your Visit Edinburgh website before final submission.

**Question cards:**

**Does it meet the brief?**  
Check whether the page is one page, about Visit Edinburgh, and suitable for the chosen audience.

**Is it readable?**  
Check headings, text size, spacing, contrast, and whether paragraphs are easy to scan.

**Do links and images work?**  
Check that links open correctly and images display clearly with suitable credits.

**Is it accessible?**  
Check image descriptions, contrast, clear language, and whether information is easy to understand.

**Side note:**  
Good testing finds problems you can actually fix.

## Slide 5: Accessibility

**Kicker:** Accessibility  
**Title:** More users can use a page when the design is clear

**Checks:**
- Clear headings show what each section is about.
- Text is readable and not too small.
- Colours have enough contrast.
- Images have useful descriptions or credits.
- Important information is not hidden in long paragraphs.
- The page still makes sense on different devices.

**Footer prompt:**  
Accessible design is good design.

## Slide 6: Feedback

**Kicker:** Feedback  
**Title:** Specific feedback leads to better refinements

**Context:**  
A peer is testing your website. Their feedback should help you decide what to improve next.

**Useful feedback examples:**

**Specific**  
"The transport section needs a clearer heading."

**Linked to users**  
"Tourists might need opening times near the top."

**Actionable**  
"Increase the contrast on this paragraph."

**Evidence-based**  
"This link did not open when I clicked it."

**Side note:**  
"It is good" is kind, but it does not tell the designer what to improve.

## Slide 7: Your Task

**Kicker:** Main Task  
**Title:** Test, collect feedback, and refine

**Task steps:**
1. Complete a test plan for your website.
2. Test readability, links, images, spelling, and accessibility.
3. Ask a peer to test your website.
4. Record feedback and evidence.
5. Choose at least two refinements.
6. Make the changes and check that they worked.

**Support:**  
Use the structure: issue, evidence, fix, retest.

**Challenge:**  
Prioritise the most important issue and justify why fixing it matters for the user.

## Slide 8: Check for Understanding

**Kicker:** Check for Understanding  
**Title:** Choose the level that stretches your thinking

**Scenario:**  
You have tested your Visit Edinburgh website and found several possible improvements.

**1 - Beginning to learn**
- Give one test for readability.
- Give one test for images.

**2 - Making progress**
- Describe one way to test links.
- Describe what to do after finding an error.

**3 - Making good progress**
- Explain the difference between identifying an issue and fixing an issue.
- Explain how you would prioritise multiple errors.

**4 - Meeting target**
- Create a mini test checklist and justify the order.
- Evaluate one change made from peer feedback and explain its impact.

**Footer prompt:**  
Start where you feel confident, then try the next level.

## Slide 9: Exit Ticket

**Kicker:** Exit Ticket  
**Title:** What evidence shows your website improved?

**1 - Beginning to learn**  
Name one issue you found.

**2 - Making progress**  
Name one issue and how you fixed it.

**3 - Making good progress**  
Name one issue, how you fixed it, and how you checked the fix worked.

**4 - Meeting target**  
Name one issue, explain your fix, and evaluate how the change improved accessibility or usability.

**Final takeaway:**  
Testing is strongest when it creates evidence for a real refinement.
